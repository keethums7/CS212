In January 2007, Wikipedia first became one of the ten most popular websites in the United States, according to Comscore Networks.[66] With 42.9 million unique visitors, it was ranked ninth, surpassing The New York Times (No. 10) and Apple (No. 11).[66] This marked a significant increase over January 2006, when Wikipedia ranked 33rd, with around 18.3 million unique visitors.[67] In 2014, it received 8 billion page views every month.[68] On February 9, 2014, The New York Times reported that Wikipedia had 18 billion page views and nearly 500 million unique visitors a month, "according to the ratings firm comScore".[69] As of March 2023[update], it ranked sixth in popularity, according to Similarweb.[70] Jeff Loveland and Joseph Reagle argue that, in process, Wikipedia follows a long tradition of historical encyclopedias that have accumulated improvements piecemeal through "stigmergic accumulation".[71][72]
